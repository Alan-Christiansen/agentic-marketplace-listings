# eBay Guidance
When a folder under `Categories/` applies, its guidance and template take precedence over the general fields below.
## Seller input
Notes, item facts, dimensions, photos, specialist terminology, or any mix. Keep the seller's meaning and any useful uncertainty.
## Identification and preparation
Identify the item as precisely as the evidence allows: maker or issuer, model or catalog reference, key specifications, condition, completeness, quantity, and included material. Flag testing, measurements, additional research, or better photos when they would materially improve value or buyer confidence. Never recommend cleaning a collectible when it could reduce value.

Recommend clear photos of the actual item: several angles, identifying marks, and close views of condition details and flaws. The main image shows the complete item on an uncluttered background and makes it obvious whether the listing is one item, a set, or a lot.
## Research and pricing
Prioritize recent verified eBay sold results. Use current asking prices and specialist markets as labeled context. Prefer genuinely comparable examples and cite sources. Adjust for condition, completeness, documented grade or certification, scarcity, demand, season, quantity, included material, fees, shipping, insurance, returns exposure, and the gap between asking and realized prices.

Record in `Research Notes`:
- comparables and sources;
- a fair-market range;
- a recommended listing format when the evidence favors fixed price or auction;
- a recommended initial price;
- a likely selling range;
- a proposed lowest acceptable price;
- price-reduction timing and amounts if interest is weak;
- a confidence level and the main uncertainties.
## General fields
Title, Price, Category, Condition, Quantity, Item Specifics, Item Description, and seller-supplied Shipping or Fulfillment. Confirm exact fields against the current listing form when they materially affect the draft.
## Drafting
Use precise, searchable language without keyword stuffing or unsupported promotional claims. Put the strongest identifying terms in the title and fill every item specific the evidence supports. Keep the title, category, condition, quantity, specifics, photos, and description consistent.

Write a clear, professional description that states exactly what is included and lets a buyer judge condition. State material flaws plainly. Include provenance only when the seller supplied it or documentation supports it. Never invent handling times, shipping methods, costs, insurance, combined-shipping terms, or return terms.
## Beyond the listing
When asked, help with buyer-question drafts, offer evaluation, bundling, packing and shipping plans, and scam avoidance. Recommend another channel when eBay is a poor fit. eBay categories, specifics, title limits, and policies change: verify the current form before relying on exact requirements.
